Air Quality Measurements

What the "from JCTsh Air Quality Monitor" numbers on a hike page actually mean.

Particulate Matter (PM1.0, PM2.5, PM4.0, PM10)

Airborne particle mass concentration, measured in micrograms per cubic meter (µg/m³), at four size cutoffs. The number names the largest particle diameter (in micrometers) counted in that bucket — so each bucket is cumulative: PM10 includes everything PM2.5 counts, plus the larger particles in between.

MeasurementWhat it captures
PM1.0Ultrafine particles — combustion smoke, some vehicle exhaust.
PM2.5The figure most health guidance cites (wildfire smoke, vehicle exhaust, cooking) — fine enough to reach deep into the lungs.
PM4.0Between PM2.5 and PM10 — a less commonly cited intermediate cutoff.
PM10Coarser particles — dust, pollen — plus everything the smaller cutoffs already counted.

There's no fixed "safe" number on this page — outdoor PM levels vary hugely by location and conditions (a stretch of trail near traffic vs. deep in a forest, a clear day vs. a smoky one). The range shown is simply what this specific hike measured.

VOC Index and NOx Index

VOC Index — volatile organic compounds (paints, cleaning products, cooking, off-gassing materials). NOx Index — nitrogen oxides (combustion: gas stoves, vehicle exhaust).

Neither is a concentration or a parts-per-million reading. Both are Sensirion's own relative index (the sensor's manufacturer), on a 1–500 scale, self-calibrating to "typical recent conditions" for wherever the sensor has been — that baseline is always mapped to 100. A value above 100 means more of that gas than usual right now (e.g. an actual VOC or combustion event); a value below 100 means less than usual (e.g. fresh air). It behaves like a nose adapting to a room's background smell, not a fixed-scale meter.

No official "low/medium/high" rating exists for this scale — unlike UV Index, which follows a real EPA/WHO standard, Sensirion does not publish universal risk bands for VOC/NOx Index (checked directly against their own documentation, 2026-09-28). The numbers shown are the raw index values for context, not a graded rating.

Both indices take a while to "learn" a baseline after the sensor powers on — readings from the first few minutes of a fresh session are less meaningful than ones taken once it's been running a while.
