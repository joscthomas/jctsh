## jctsh

A personal hobby, first and foremost — for Joseph, with Robin benefiting secondarily
from specific features and generally supportive of the time spent on it. It grew from a
decade of general tech interest (a SmartThings hub, basic household sensors) into
something much deeper over the last year: hands-on ESP32 firmware, circuit design, and
3D printing, feeding a lifelong pull toward building tools and tinkering with
electronics that goes back to a career that started in programming before moving into
project/program management.

The core satisfaction is the puzzle itself — multi-dimensional, spanning hardware,
firmware, and now AI-assisted development — and the specific pleasure of
problem-solving through to something that actually works. A meaningful thread running
through the most-developed parts of jctsh is the outdoors: Joseph hikes several times a
week, and building gadgets to bring on those hikes (`hiking-monitor`,
`air-quality-monitor`, `hike-izer`) is a distinct, especially strong source of
enjoyment, not incidental to the rest.

A second purpose emerged from the first, initially by accident and now deliberately:
writing ESP32 firmware became the on-ramp to understanding AI-assisted development, and
that's turned into a real epiphany — using AI/Claude Code to build out a personal
"operating system" (echoing jctsh's own `tos/` naming) is now an explicit, exciting
direction in its own right, not just a side effect of the hobby.

Not everything here is "critical" in a load-bearing sense (the garage door automation
isn't essential infrastructure), but several things have become genuinely,
daily-enjoyed conveniences regardless — the line between "hobby project" and "thing we
actually rely on and like using" isn't as clean as it might sound.
